Perspectives

Broad ideas that guide how I approach the relationship between medicine and data.

Connecting medical questions with analytical methods

A useful analysis begins with a clear question. I want to learn how to translate medical questions into suitable variables, comparisons, and analytical steps, while keeping the original context in view.

Learning from public data

Public datasets offer opportunities to practise analytical methods and examine different study designs. I value understanding how data were collected, what they represent, and where their limitations lie before choosing a method.

Looking beyond a single result

A figure, a statistical estimate, or a model score is part of a larger argument. I aim to consider uncertainty, alternative explanations, and the scope of a conclusion when interpreting an analysis.

Questions & Reflections

Questions I keep in mind while learning to work with data.

What makes a question suitable for data analysis?

I try to ask whether the available data match the question, which variables are needed, and what kind of conclusion the study design can reasonably support.

How can a result be convincing?

I want to understand the assumptions behind an analysis, compare plausible explanations, and check whether the result remains useful under different analytical choices.

How can an analysis be communicated clearly?

I aim to explain the question, methods, findings, and limitations in a logical order, with readable figures and enough documentation for others to follow the work.
